# Technical Writing

- Follow these rules while composing the requested output.
- Match the audience, purpose, requested language, and required format.

## Scope and project context

- Apply the STE rules below to English technical prose unless the user requests principles-only writing.
- For other languages, apply the general clarity rules without claiming ASD-STE100 compliance.
- Read applicable project instructions, terminology, and document requirements.
- Use existing project sources without requiring a specific filename or format.
- Keep project terminology and examples outside this global skill.
- Do not create project files unless requested or required for the task.

## Project glossary

- Use relevant entries from the supplied or identified project glossary when available.
- Otherwise, use established terminology from project sources or the subject field.
- Do not invent definitions or infer STE permission from usage, glossary inclusion, or review status.
- Treat unresolved terms as unverified.
- Create or update glossary files only when requested or required.

## Meaning and terminology

- Establish meaning from the request and relevant sources.
- Do not add unsupported facts or actions.
- Use consistent project terminology or established subject terminology.
- Prefer established terms and acronyms when they preserve meaning and the intended reader understands them.
- Explain unfamiliar terms and abbreviations when readers need them.
- Preserve uncertainty, requirement strength, exceptions, quantities,
  units, sequence, authorization, and completion criteria.
- Preserve exact code, commands, identifiers, API fields, UI labels,
  quotations, and links.
- Ask for clarification only when ambiguity materially affects the result.

## Write and structure

- Lead with the answer, result, or required action.
- Keep output lean and concise while preserving all information needed to complete the task.
- Use short, complete sentences with one main idea.
- Use active voice and identify the actor when relevant.
- Make pronoun references clear.
- Repeat the applicable name when needed.
- Use one consistent term for each concept.
- Avoid idioms, figurative language, unnecessary jargon, and ambiguous
  noun groups.
- Organize each paragraph around one topic.
- Present information in the order readers need it.
- Use headings, lists, and tables only when they improve clarity.
- Restructure sentences when word substitution would obscure meaning.
- Keep the detail proportional to the task and audience.
- Replace vague benefits and figurative descriptions with concrete actions, mechanisms, or supported results.
- Support attributed claims with named sources, or remove the claims.
- Remove filler, stock framing, and generic conclusions that add no information.

## Instruction authoring

When creating or editing `AGENTS.md` or `SKILL.md`:

- Write each rule as a direct, actionable instruction.
- Give each sentence its own bullet.
- Use the fewest words that preserve the full requirement.

## Procedures

- Give actions as direct instructions.
- Give each action its own sentence, except for simultaneous actions.
- Put prerequisites and conditions before the applicable action.
- Make the sequence and completion conditions clear.
- Keep required actions, requirements, and limits in the procedure,
  rather than in notes.
- Verify that readers can complete the procedure without its notes.

## Safety instructions

- Put precautions before the hazardous action.
- State the required action.
- When supplied or verified information is available, explain the hazard or consequence.
- Use the applicable safety classification and project conventions.
- Preserve controlled or legally required wording.
- Report conflicts instead of changing protected wording silently.

## STE rules

- Use the project-required edition, or the current edition if none is specified.
- Identify an accessible complete official reference for the applicable edition.
- Use a matching supplied or local reference, or obtain it from the official source.
- Consult applicable rules and needed dictionary entries in that reference.
- Reuse verified decisions while their edition, meaning, grammatical role, and context remain applicable.
- Identify procedural, descriptive, note, and safety text.
- Apply all applicable rules across the nine writing-rule sections.
- Apply relevant general recommendations.
- Use approved words only with their permitted meanings, grammatical roles, and forms.
- Use technical nouns and technical verbs only as permitted by the standard.
- Prefer accurate approved dictionary wording over unnecessary technical verbs.
- Permit noun and verb uses of the same term only when each independently qualifies.
- Establish each technical term's meaning, grammatical role, applicable STE category, and source.
- Use existing project terminology records when available.
- Keep terminology decisions within the active project or task.
- Treat unfamiliar terms as unresolved until their permitted use is established.
- Use a clear alternative for unresolved terms when exact text is not required.
- Do not treat project usage alone as permission to use a term.
- Use permitted infinitives, imperatives, simple present/past/future tenses, and past participles as adjectives.
- Use verb-derived “-ing” forms only as permitted technical nouns or modifiers within technical nouns.
- Preserve independently approved dictionary words ending in “-ing”.
- Use phrasal verbs only with permitted meanings.
- Limit ordinary multi-word nouns to three words.
- Preserve longer official technical nouns and clarify them using Rule 2.2.
- Use American spelling unless official directives require otherwise.
- Do not omit necessary words or articles to shorten sentences.
- Do not use contractions or semicolons in STE prose.
- Use passive voice in descriptions only when the actor is unknown.
- Apply the official word-counting rules and exceptions.
- Count vertical-list introductions and items separately under Rule 8.4.
- Count parenthetical text as one outer word and check its internal text separately under Rule 8.5.
- Apply Rule 8.6 to numbers, units, abbreviations, identifiers, quotations, titles, labels, and specified proper nouns.
- Count hyphenated words under Rule 8.7 without inventing hyphenation to evade limits.
- Limit procedural and safety sentences to 20 words, subject to the standard's exceptions.
- Limit descriptive sentences and notes to 25 words, subject to the standard's exceptions.
- Limit descriptive paragraphs to one topic and six sentences.
- Apply the standard's relevant provisions to exact text preserved under these instructions.
- Do not treat preservation of exact text as evidence that its STE use is permitted.
- Report conflicts without silently changing protected text.
- If the complete reference is unavailable, use the general clarity rules and known STE rules without claiming full compliance.
- Disclose reference gaps when strict verification is requested.

## Before sending

- Correct remaining violations before returning the requested output.
- Preserve technical meaning, requirements, and uncertainty during correction.
- Keep routine writing checks out of the response.
- Include explanations, alternatives, or review comments when requested.

## Full-compliance claims

- Complete these checks before claiming full compliance or when the user requests a formal compliance review.
- Identify the reviewed scope, edition, and official reference used.
- Verify the applicable writing rules and dictionary entries against the complete official reference.
- Use automated checks where they provide reliable evidence.
- Review meaning, technical accuracy, terminology, and applicable rules beyond automated checks.
- Correct identified violations and check the affected text again.
- Report completed checks and unresolved findings with rule references where available.
- State that compliance could not be verified when references, terminology decisions, or required checks remain unresolved.
- Do not treat readability scores, automated checks, or model judgment alone as proof of full compliance.
- Obtain informed human review before claiming full compliance.
- Treat human review as a local assurance requirement, not an ASD-STE100 requirement.
