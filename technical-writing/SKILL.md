# Technical Writing

## Essential authoring rules

- Use the fewest words that preserve clarity, accuracy, completeness, and the full requirement.
- Use plain English, familiar words, direct verbs, and short, complete sentences.
- Write model instructions as direct, actionable rules with one sentence per bullet when the required format permits.
- Prefer established shorthand when readers understand it and applicable rules permit it.
- Define unfamiliar terms or abbreviations once and use them consistently.
- Preserve scope, conditions, exceptions, uncertainty, requirement strength, quantities, units, sequence, authorization boundaries, and completion criteria.
- Preserve exact code, commands, identifiers, API fields, UI labels, quotations, links, and controlled or legally required wording.
- Remove repetition, filler, stock framing, unnecessary jargon, figurative language, and generic conclusions.
- State each requirement once and group related rules where readers need them.
- Keep instructions proportional to the task without adding unsupported facts, actions, or requirements.

## Scope and context

- Use when creating, revising, or reviewing model prompts, `AGENTS.md` rules, or skill instructions, including instructions outside these files.
- Do not activate solely because a task reads `AGENTS.md` or uses another skill.
- For other prose, use this skill when the user requests it or ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English (STE) guidance.
- Apply the included Issue 9 guidance to English prose unless the user requests principles-only writing.
- For other languages, apply the clarity rules without claiming STE compliance.
- Apply these instructions to the requested output without treating them as a request to change policies.
- Match the audience, purpose, language, format, and applicable project requirements.
- Read applicable instructions, terminology, glossaries, and document requirements without requiring a specific filename or format.
- Keep project terms, examples, and terminology decisions in their owning project or task.
- Create project or glossary files only when requested or required.
- Ask for clarification only when ambiguity materially affects the result.

## Clarity and structure

- Lead with the answer, result, or required action.
- Present information gradually and in the order readers need it.
- Keep each sentence to one main idea and each paragraph to one topic.
- Use active voice and identify the actor when relevant.
- Use passive voice in STE descriptions only when the actor is unknown.
- Use one consistent term per concept and consistent wording for equivalent instructions.
- Make pronoun references clear and repeat names when needed.
- Use headings, lists, and tables only when they improve clarity.
- Use concrete actions, mechanisms, or supported results instead of vague benefits.
- Support attributed claims with named sources, or remove them.
- Keep necessary words, articles, and connecting words that prevent ambiguity.
- Prefer English phrases over unfamiliar Latin abbreviations, and omit unnecessary examples or abbreviations.
- Restructure sentences when word substitution would obscure meaning or change requirement strength.

## STE words and grammar

- Use approved words only with their permitted meanings, grammatical roles, and forms.
- Prefer approved verbs that describe actions directly over unnecessary noun phrases or technical verbs.
- Use technical nouns and verbs only when their subject categories and context permit them.
- Establish each technical term's meaning, grammatical role, applicable category, and source from project or subject terminology.
- Do not infer STE permission from project usage, glossary inclusion, or review status alone.
- Permit noun and verb uses of the same term only when each independently qualifies.
- Treat unfamiliar terms as unresolved and use clear alternatives when exact text is not required.
- Treat suggested word alternatives as context-dependent choices, not automatic substitutions.
- Use permitted infinitives, imperatives, simple present/past/future tenses, and past participles as adjectives.
- Use verb-derived “-ing” forms only as permitted technical nouns or modifiers within technical nouns.
- Preserve independently approved dictionary words ending in “-ing”.
- Use phrasal verbs only with permitted meanings.
- Limit ordinary multi-word nouns to three words.
- Introduce longer official technical nouns in full, then use clear shorter forms or hyphens that clarify related words.
- Prevent dense sequences of abbreviations.
- Use American spelling unless official directives require otherwise.
- Do not use contractions or semicolons in STE prose.
- Preserve protected wording and report conflicts instead of silently changing it.
- Do not infer STE permission merely because exact text must remain unchanged.

## Procedures and safety

- Apply these procedure and safety principles even in principles-only mode.
- Give actions as direct instructions, with one action per sentence except for simultaneous actions.
- Put prerequisites and conditions before the applicable action and make sequence and completion criteria clear.
- Keep required actions, requirements, and limits in procedures rather than notes.
- Verify that readers can complete procedures without their notes.
- Put precautions before hazardous actions and state the required action.
- Explain supplied or verified hazards and consequences using applicable safety classifications and project conventions.

## STE sentence limits and word counts

- Identify procedural, descriptive, note, and safety text before applying sentence limits.
- Limit procedural and safety sentences to 20 words and descriptive sentences and notes to 25 words, subject to STE counting exceptions.
- Limit descriptive paragraphs to six sentences.
- Count a vertical-list introduction and each item separately, with the introductory colon ending the introduction.
- Count parenthetical text as one outer word and check its internal text separately.
- Count each number, number with its unit, abbreviation, and alphanumeric identifier as one word.
- Count quoted text, titles, headings, placards, labels, and proper nouns for persons, groups, organizations, and geopolitical entities as one word.
- Exclude paragraph and step numbers from sentence counts.
- Count hyphenated words as one word without inventing hyphenation to evade limits.

## Before delivery

- Correct remaining violations without changing technical meaning, requirements, or uncertainty.
- Reuse verified decisions while their edition, meaning, grammatical role, and context remain applicable.
- Keep routine writing checks out of the response and include review comments or alternatives when requested.
- Treat this skill as condensed guidance, not a complete dictionary or proof of full STE compliance.
- Use automated checks only when they provide reliable evidence and review meaning, terminology, and technical accuracy separately.
- For formal compliance reviews, identify the scope, edition, verified criteria, and unresolved rules, terminology permissions, or project-edition differences.
- Report unresolved verification gaps instead of claiming full compliance from this skill, readability scores, automated checks, or model judgment alone.
- Obtain informed human review before claiming full compliance as a local assurance requirement, not an ASD-STE100 requirement.
